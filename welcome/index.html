Three questions drivers always get wrong

Most drivers either overpay, or get a January shock. Kerb answers all three, live, as you log your work.

“How much tax will I owe?”

Kerb applies the real 2026/27 rates — Income Tax, Class 4 at 6%/2%, Class 2, the £1,000 trading allowance — and shows the figure as it grows.

“What can I actually claim?”

It claims your mileage at 55p/25p, or your real running costs — whichever leaves you better off — and tells you which to use.

“How much is safe to spend?”

Your tax pot and your spendable profit, split out separately, so the money you owe HMRC is never money you've already spent.

“What about my other job?”

If you're also employed, Kerb keeps PAYE separate and stacks your self-employment on top correctly — so the tax figure is right, not doubled.

Honest answers

Does Kerb file my tax return for me?

No. Kerb is not HMRC-recognised filing software and cannot submit anything to HMRC. It prepares your figures and tells you which box each one goes in; you file through HMRC's own online service.

Is this tax advice?

No. Kerb gives estimates to help you budget and keep records. For anything unusual or significant, speak to a qualified accountant.

Do I need an account?

No. Open it and start — there's nothing to sign up for, because there's no server to sign up to.

Does it work without signal?

Yes. Add Kerb to your Home Screen and it works fully offline — useful in car parks and dead spots.

What if I get a new phone?

Export a backup file from Settings and import it on the new device. Because nothing is stored on a server, that backup is your copy.

I'm employed as well as delivering. Does that work?

Yes — that's built in. Add your payslips or your salary and Kerb stacks your self-employment on top, so you're only setting aside what you genuinely owe.

Please read: Kerb provides estimates and record-keeping to help you prepare your own Self Assessment. It is not tax, accounting or legal advice, and it is not HMRC-recognised filing software.
